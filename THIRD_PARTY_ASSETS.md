The following free Standard editions were downloaded from the artist's official pages. Original license notices are retained in `assets/characters/*-LICENSE.txt`.

Local adaptations: mesh selection, head/neck and leg cropping, facial/waist adjustments, skin/hair/costume material changes, boots/shorts material regions, custom cloth face mask and cape, weapon attachment, animation retargeting and layered aiming. Textures were resized and encoded as WebP for delivery; duplicate textures were consolidated. The underlying character meshes and source animations are by Quaternius, not original project artwork.

In v108, Wraith reuses the existing Male Ranger outfit, male base character, simple parted hair, and retargeted animation library as a visible-faced twilight traveller. Local adaptations hide the original pauldron and hood, tint the cloth and leather, recolor the short hair silver-gray, and add a project-generated short scarf, moon clasp, cape, and shadow weapon displays. The former procedural `wraith-model.js` character was replaced by the shared skinned-character system. No new external assets or dependencies were introduced for this replacement.

In v109, Wraith retains that full character rig and animation system, re-enables the original skinned Ranger hood, and omits the short hair. Local adaptations add a fitted cool-gray death mask with dark eye sockets and narrow cyan eye slits, darken the clothing to black-violet, lengthen the project-generated cape with an irregular torn hem, and adjust the shadow weapon palette. The hood retains its authored skin weights; the new mask and cape details are generated by project code. This change uses the same existing CC0 assets and introduces no new external assets or dependencies.

In v110, Wraith uses the subtitle RIFT WALKER. Project-generated surface details replace the skull-like mask with a black cloth veil, a single asymmetric moon-white fissure, and subdued eye lines. Local adaptations use ink-black and blue-gray cloth, a small broken silver clasp, and an obliquely torn cape hem. The generated weapons use compressed dark fragments, discontinuous pale blade edges, and dark book pages with narrow white symbols; shadow combat effects use black silhouettes and pale cuts. The existing Quaternius character rig, hood, and animation assets remain in use. These are local code and palette changes with no new external assets or dependencies.

In v112, the same Wraith character has three weapon-dependent appearances while retaining the rounded Ranger hood and fractured cloth mask: a traveller with a narrow long scarf, diagonal chest cloth and hand-local shadow traces for Shade; an assassin with short shoulder cloth, a short cape and waist/back trails for Shadowblade; and a mage with an open V-shaped collar, a long cape, split front robe panels and slowly rising hem traces for Grimoire. The front robe panels attach at the pelvis, remain gravity-aligned, and sway lightly with the stride instead of being rigidly attached to the thighs. The scarf, collar, cape, robe panels and soft shadow shaders are local project-generated adaptations in `shadow-appearance.js`, `skinned-hero.js` and `shadow-aura.js`. They reuse the existing Quaternius CC0 body, outfit and animation assets; no new external or paid assets were added. These are equipment appearances, not new heroes or weapons: the roster remains seven heroes and thirteen weapons, with unchanged combat values and upgrade rules.

The hashed delivery GLBs use lossless gzip compression. Baked animation tracks preserve every retargeted Float32 sample. Original source assets and an uncompressed compatibility path are retained.

The game does not contain extracted models, textures or audio from Ori, NieR, Chainsaw Man, Rozen Maiden, or other commercial reference works.

Each Fantasy Choir piece has a 1.5-second end-to-start crossfade to prepare it for repeated playback. All five recordings were resampled to 32 kHz stereo, processed with a target of −21 LUFS integrated loudness and a −2 dBTP true-peak ceiling, and encoded to the listed Ogg or MP3 delivery format. These are processing targets, not a claim that lossy output measures exactly those values. The delivered recordings total 7,499,280 bytes (about 7.5 MB).

The game uses these recordings for forest, snow, ash, coast, and high-pressure/boss music, with fades between selections. The procedural score remains available for the sand biome and as a loading or failure fallback. Sources and adaptations are documented for provenance; this record does not claim a completed subjective listening review.

Each source page declares CC0 (the battle/bow pack offers CC0 among its license choices). The rifle/shotgun reports are designed from rubberduck’s recorded fireworks, not recordings of actual firearms.

Adaptations: onset editing, material layering, selected reverse textures for shadow magic, low/high-pass filtering, fades, per-role level matching, and mono 32 kHz MP3 encoding. Multiple takes use different source segments/layers; recorded playback does not randomly change pitch. `scripts/build-material-sfx.py` reproduces the atlas with Python standard library and FFmpeg from the downloaded source packages. [Machine-readable source and recipe record](docs/audio-material-sources.json) lists selected files, hashes, full source URLs, and exact transformations. Original source packs remain outside the shipping game.

The v94 edit shortens handheld weapon reports and recovery cues, separates pull/latch stages, removes contact layers from harpoon air swings, and uses breath/air textures with quieter wet accents for lanterns. Shadow magic keeps paper only for page handling. Per-cue playback gains range from 0.32 to 1. The current mono 32 kHz atlas uses 128 kbps MP3 encoding (910,124 bytes, 56.8 seconds, 130 unique edits and 70 lookup keys including aliases). These are new edits of the same licensed sources, not new claims of field recording or subjective listening approval.

## Ground and stone surface textures — CC0 1.0

The four files in `assets/environment/` are unmodified 1K JPG maps from Poly Haven: [Forest Floor](https://polyhaven.com/a/forest_floor) (eye-candy.xyz) and [Rock 01](https://polyhaven.com/a/rock_01), distributed under [CC0](https://polyhaven.com/license). Source pages and file checksums were checked on 2026-10-10 and recorded in `assets/environment/sources.json`.

Each set supplies its diffuse and displacement map. Runtime shaders mix the diffuse map into the existing biome palette and use the displacement map only for surface-normal relief: playable terrain is not displaced. Forest litter is limited to forest/organic coastal and confluence ground; stone uses blended projections on obstacle boulders and selected ruin stones. These are real surface textures, not scanned environment models. Files are served locally, shared across maps and included in the offline bundle; failed image loading retains the previous procedural surface. Total delivered images: 2,880,466 bytes.
